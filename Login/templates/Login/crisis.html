What to Expect When You Call

Reaching out for the first time can feel scary. Here's what typically happens:

1

A trained listener picks up

You don't need to introduce yourself or share your name unless you want to. Conversations are confidential.

2

You talk at your own pace

You don't need to have everything figured out. Even saying "I don't know what's wrong, I just feel bad" is enough.

3

You'll get support and options

The counselor will listen without judgment, help you understand what you're feeling, and suggest next steps if you want them.

Warning Signs to Take Seriously

Please reach out for help if you or someone you know is experiencing:

  • Thoughts of suicide, self-harm, or wanting to "disappear"
  • Feeling completely hopeless or that nothing will ever get better
  • Inability to perform daily tasks (eating, sleeping, attending classes)
  • Severe panic attacks or constant overwhelming anxiety
  • Hearing or seeing things others don't
  • Sudden, drastic changes in mood, behavior, or personality
  • Substance abuse as a coping mechanism

Supporting a Friend in Crisis

If you're worried about someone:

  • Listen without trying to fix. Sometimes people just need to be heard.
  • Take it seriously. Don't dismiss their feelings as "just a phase."
  • Ask directly. Asking "are you thinking about hurting yourself?" does not plant the idea — research shows it actually reduces risk.
  • Stay with them if they are in immediate danger and call a helpline together.
  • Encourage professional help, but don't force it. You can offer to go with them.

About This Service

Student Companion is an AI-powered tool designed to help you understand and track your mental wellness. It is not a replacement for professional mental health care, therapy, or crisis intervention.

If you are in crisis, please contact one of the helplines above. They are staffed by trained professionals who can provide immediate, human support that AI cannot.
